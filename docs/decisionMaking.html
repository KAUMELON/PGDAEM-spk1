1  Decision making in agriculture and extension organisations

This chapter introduces decision making as a process, examines how decisions are made on farms and in agricultural extension organisations, and classifies agricultural decisions by time horizon, complexity and uncertainty.

1.1 What is decision making?

Decision making is the cognitive, rational process of selecting the best course of action from multiple alternatives to achieve a specific goal. It involves identifying problems, evaluating options and committing to a path of action (Van den Ban & Hawkins, 1996). In agriculture, this process is shaped not only by economics but also by personal experience, social influences and access to information.

1.2 The seven-step decision-making process

Decision making can be described as a sequence of seven steps (Figure 1.1):

  1. Define the problem: clearly identify the issue requiring resolution or the goal to be achieved.
  2. Gather relevant information: collect internal and external data, including market research, expert opinions and past experiences.
  3. Identify alternatives: develop a range of possible, actionable solutions or options.
  4. Weigh the evidence: evaluate the pros and cons of each alternative, assessing potential risks and benefits.
  5. Choose the best option: select the most suitable alternative that best fulfils the objective.
  6. Take action: put the chosen plan into action by allocating resources and assigning tasks.
  7. Review the decision: evaluate the outcome, analyse consequences and determine whether the problem was resolved.

1.3 Decision making in agriculture

Agricultural decision making is a complex process influenced by economic, environmental and social factors. It is a behavioural process in which farmers and agribusiness stakeholders evaluate available information, consider alternatives and choose actions that best align with their goals (Van den Ban & Hawkins, 1996). Decision making in farm households is not solely the responsibility of the male head — other family members may be involved, and some decisions are made collectively.

One critical decision is whether to adopt new technologies: improved seeds, fertilisers, agrochemicals and mechanisation. Determining profitability requires farm-specific information (such as soil tests) as well as insights from neighbouring farmers who have already adopted.

1.4 Decision making in agricultural extension organisations

Decision making in extension organisations is inherently complex because of diverse farmer needs, variable environmental conditions, evolving market dynamics and policy constraints (Leeuwis & Van den Ban, 2004). An Agricultural Decision Making Support System (ADSS) integrates agricultural information systems, simulation models and expert systems to enable extension agents to deliver precise, real-time and customised guidance. Decision support systems are discussed further in Section 4.6.

1.5.2 Classification by complexity

  • Routine (low complexity): repetitive, everyday decisions made from habit, past experience or standard practice. They require little new information or analysis.
  • Tactical (moderate complexity): decisions that require some planning and assessment, affect operations for a season or a few years, and involve evaluating different options.
  • Strategic (high complexity): long-term, high-impact decisions that require significant information gathering, expert consultation and analysis, and involve large financial investments with lasting consequences.

Quick revision

  • Decision making → selecting the best course of action among alternatives to reach a goal.
  • Seven steps → define, gather information, identify alternatives, weigh evidence, choose, act, review.
  • Farm decisions → shaped by economics, experience, social influence and information; often made collectively in the household.
  • ADSS → information systems + simulation models + expert systems for customised advice.
  • By time → strategic (5+ years), tactical (1–5 years), operational (days–weeks).
  • By complexity → routine, tactical, strategic.
  • By uncertainty → certain, risk-based, uncertain.
